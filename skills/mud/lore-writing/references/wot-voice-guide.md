# WoT voice guide

What it sounds like when the prose is right, and the smells of when
it isn't.

## Jordan tells (use sparingly)

- The Wheel of Time turns / the Wheel weaves as the Wheel wills /
  there are no beginnings nor endings. Use **once per major scene
  opener at most.** Anything more reads as parody.
- Smoothing skirts as a tell of feminine reserve. Universally used
  by women across nations to signal composure or mild irritation.
- Tugging braids — distinctly Two Rivers (Egwene/Nynaeve).
- "Sniffing" as terse contempt — Cadsuane, Wise Ones.
- "Light" as exclamation, oath fragment, or noun for the divine.

## Sentence rhythm

- Comfortable with long sentences threaded with em-dashes and
  semicolons. Short punchy sentences land harder for the same reason.
- Description layers from broad to specific: room → object → detail
  → person.
- Internal monologue is comma-spliced — characters' thoughts feel
  hurried and unstoppered.

## Things to avoid

- **Modern slang.** "Cool", "okay", "guys", "totally" — all break
  the register.
- **Modern technology metaphors.** No clockwork, no machinery, no
  optical analogies past simple lenses.
- **Tolkien crossover words.** No "fey", no "sward" — these belong
  to a different register.
- **Snark-narrator.** The narrator does not wink at the reader.
  Characters can be wry; the prose is sincere.
- **Too much italicization.** Jordan italicizes for *one* word at a
  time, very rarely two. Avoid italicized phrases.

## Tone calibration

| Surface | Register |
|---|---|
| Room desc | Sincere, sensory, unhurried |
| Item desc | Specific; weight or use detail; one telling adjective |
| NPC dialogue | Voice-driven; clipped or layered per nation/faction |
| Help topic | Plainspoken; meta-information from the game, not in-world |
| MOTD entry | Author voice, not Jordan voice — break frame is OK |
| In-world book | Full Jordan register — long, layered, period-appropriate |
